01 · Overview

A practical guide to the Voyage AI model family

Voyage AI (a MongoDB company) builds embedding models and rerankers — the two components that determine retrieval accuracy in semantic search, RAG, and agentic systems. The four product categories

Text embeddings

General-purpose vectorization for search and RAG. The Voyage 4 family (voyage-4-large, voyage-4, voyage-4-lite, open-weight voyage-4-nano) shares one embedding space — an industry first — so you can mix models between the query and document sides ("asymmetric retrieval") without ever re-indexing.

Domain-specific embeddings

voyage-code-3 (code), voyage-finance-2 (finance), voyage-law-2 (legal). Embedding models are parameter-constrained by latency budgets, so dedicating capacity to one domain delivers outsized accuracy in that domain. Harvey, the legal AI platform, partnered with Voyage for custom legal embeddings.

Contextualized chunk & multimodal embeddings

voyage-context-3 embeds every chunk with awareness of the full document — replacing manual context-augmentation pipelines. voyage-multimodal-3.5 embeds interleaved text, images, PDF screenshots, and video through a single backbone, avoiding the "modality gap" that affects CLIP-style models.

Rerankers

Cross-encoders that re-score your initial search results for a precision boost. rerank-2.5 and rerank-2.5-lite add instruction following: steer relevance with natural language ("prioritize regulatory documents, not court cases").

Why it matters: retrieval quality — not the LLM — is usually the bottleneck in RAG accuracy and hallucination rate. Upgrading the embedding model and adding a reranker are drop-in API changes that lift the whole system without touching the rest of your stack.

Platform & cost facts worth knowing

  • Access: Voyage API, MongoDB Atlas Embedding & Reranking API, GCP Vertex AI Model Garden, AWS Marketplace, Azure Managed Applications, and Snowflake. VPC deployment is supported for teams with data-residency requirements.
  • Batch API: 33% discount versus standard endpoints with a 12-hour completion window — ideal for large initial ingestion jobs.
  • Cost-efficiency features: Matryoshka embeddings (2048/1024/512/256 dimensions) plus quantization (float, int8, binary) can cut vector-database storage by up to ~99% with minimal accuracy loss.
  • Free tier: the first 200M tokens are free on all current models (plus 150B pixels on multimodal models).

03 · Model-by-Model Breakdown

Model cards

A closer look at each current model — what it is, what makes it different, who it fits, and the honest considerations to weigh before choosing it.

voyage-4-large
Flagship Text

Overview. The first production-grade mixture-of-experts embedding model. The current state of the art for general-purpose and multilingual retrieval; it tops the RTEB leaderboard and supersedes voyage-3-large at a lower price ($0.12 vs $0.18 per 1M tokens).

What's different
MoE architecture (40% lower serving cost vs comparable dense models); shared 4-series embedding space; 32K context; Matryoshka dimensions + binary/int8 quantization
Best fit
Teams that put accuracy first: enterprise knowledge bases, premium search products, agent memory at scale
Example workloads
High-fidelity document indexing, multilingual enterprise search, context-engineered agents

Why it stands out. +14.05% over OpenAI v3 Large and +8.20% over Cohere Embed v4 on 29 RTEB datasets — and documents indexed with it can be queried by cheaper 4-series models (asymmetric retrieval), so flagship-grade indexing doesn't commit you to flagship query costs.

Considerations. Twice the price of voyage-4. If cost is the priority, start with voyage-4-lite queries over a voyage-4-large index.

voyage-4 & voyage-4-lite
Active Text

Overview. The mid and efficiency tiers of the 4 family. voyage-4 ($0.06/M) approaches voyage-3-large quality; voyage-4-lite ($0.02/M) approaches voyage-3.5 quality at a fraction of the compute, with the highest per-request throughput (1M tokens).

What's different
The same shared embedding space as the flagship — upgrade or downgrade your query model at any time without re-indexing
Best fit
High-QPS production search, cost-sensitive RAG, teams scaling from prototype to production
Example workloads
Query-side embedding for chatbots, agent shared-memory reads, log and ticket search

Why it stands out. Voyage's recommended pattern — index with 4-large, query with 4-lite — outperforms symmetric retrieval with small models across the board, while keeping per-query cost at the $0.02 tier.

Considerations. Standalone (symmetric) accuracy trails 4-large by roughly 1.9–4.8% on average.

voyage-4-nano
Open weights Text

Overview. Voyage's first open-weights model, available on Hugging Face under Apache 2.0 — and compatible with the entire 4-series embedding space.

What's different
Free local inference that interoperates with the hosted 4-series models
Best fit
Local prototyping, air-gapped or edge experimentation, open-source-first teams
Example workloads
Local development → production handoff: prototype with nano, then serve queries on the same index with lite/4/large

Why it stands out. You can experiment completely offline and move to production without re-embedding anything — the vectors are compatible.

Considerations. Lowest accuracy in the family; designed as the entry point rather than the production document indexer.

voyage-context-3
Active Contextualized chunks

Overview. Contextualized chunk embeddings: the model reads the whole document in one pass and emits one vector per chunk that encodes both the chunk's detail and the document's global context — no metadata prepending, no LLM-generated context summaries.

What's different
A drop-in replacement (same vector count and dimensions as standard embeddings, unlike ColBERT); reduces sensitivity to chunking strategy (2.06% variance vs 4.34% for voyage-3-large)
Best fit
Legal, financial, medical, and research teams working with long unstructured documents where missing context is costly
Example workloads
Contract clause retrieval ("which encryption does Client X require?"), 10-K analysis, white-paper RAG, queries that span multiple sections

Why it stands out. +14.24% chunk-level vs OpenAI-v3-large, and +6.76% over LLM-based contextual-retrieval pipelines while removing that entire engineering step. Binary-512 embeddings match OpenAI float-3072 quality at roughly 0.5% of the vector-database storage cost.

Considerations. The highest text price ($0.18/M); documents must be submitted as ordered chunk sequences.

voyage-multimodal-3.5
Active Multimodal

Overview. One encoder for interleaved text, images, screenshots, PDFs, tables, figures — and video frames. The first production-grade video embedding model with Matryoshka dimensions.

What's different
A single-backbone architecture eliminates the CLIP "modality gap"; "screenshots are all you need" — no parsing, OCR, or layout-analysis pipeline
Best fit
Teams with visual-heavy corpora — slides, scanned PDFs, dashboards — or video libraries to make searchable
Example workloads
Text-to-video scene search, slide-deck retrieval, screenshot-based document RAG, mixed text+image knowledge bases

Why it stands out. Outperforms Cohere Embed v4 (+2.26%), Amazon Nova 2 (+8.38%), and Google Multimodal 001 (+30.57%) on visual document retrieval, and +4.65% over Google on video at ~6x lower cost — while staying within 0.29% of voyage-3-large on pure text, so one model can serve your whole corpus.

Considerations. Pixel-based pricing is worth estimating up front for image/video-heavy corpora; long videos should be split into scenes of ≤32K tokens.

voyage-code-3
Active Domain · Code

Overview. Voyage's purpose-built code retrieval model, widely adopted by code assistant and agent builders.

What's different
Trained on trillions of tokens with docstring↔code and code↔code pairs across 300+ languages; 32K context (vs OpenAI's 8K and CodeSage's 1K); evaluated on a contamination-aware benchmark suite
Best fit
Code assistants, coding agents, internal monorepo search, developer tools
Example workloads
Text-to-code, code-to-code similarity, docstring-to-code, repository-scale semantic search

Why it stands out. +13.80% vs OpenAI-v3-large and +16.81% vs CodeSage-large over 32 code datasets; binary-256 embeddings still beat OpenAI float-3072 by 4.81% at 1/384 the storage.

Considerations. Premium tier ($0.18/M). For mixed code+prose corpora, voyage-4-large is strong across domains — though code remains code-3's specialty.

voyage-finance-2
Active Domain · Finance

Overview. Finance-tuned embeddings for filings, financial news, advice, and report QA — including hybrid tabular + text reasoning (TAT-QA-style data).

What's different
32K context fits full filing sections; trained for numeric and tabular-text hybrids
Best fit
Fintech research tools, investment analytics, compliance search
Example workloads
10-K / FinanceBench-style QA, earnings-call retrieval, personal-finance assistants

Why it stands out. +7% over OpenAI (the next-best model) and +12% over Cohere across 11 finance datasets, none of which were seen during training.

Considerations. Fixed 1024 dimensions without quantization options. voyage-4-large now matches or exceeds the series-2 domain models on their own domains, so it's worth benchmarking both on your data.

voyage-law-2
Active Domain · Legal

Overview. Legal-tuned embeddings trained on roughly 1T additional legal tokens with an emphasis on long documents. Topped the MTEB legal-retrieval leaderboard at launch.

What's different
Strong on multilingual case law (Chinese LeCaRDv2, German LegalQuAD/GerDaLIR); long-context retrieval improved across all domains, not just law
Best fit
Legal-tech platforms, law-firm knowledge systems, e-discovery, contract intelligence
Example workloads
Precedent retrieval, statute matching, contract QA

Why it stands out. +6% average over OpenAI v3 large on 8 legal datasets, and more than +10% on three of them — in a domain where retrieval misses are expensive.

Considerations. 16K context (below the current 32K standard) and fixed 1024 dimensions. For new legal builds, it's worth benchmarking voyage-4-large and voyage-context-3 alongside it.

rerank-2.5
Active Reranker

Overview. Voyage's quality-flagship cross-encoder, with first-in-family instruction following and a 32K context window (8x Cohere Rerank v3.5's).

What's different
Steer relevance in natural language ("retrieve statutes, not case law"); distilled from larger in-house instruction-following models; outperforms LLM-as-reranker setups
Best fit
Any existing search stack — it layers on top of BM25, OpenAI, Cohere, or Voyage embeddings without migration
Example workloads
Precision boost on top-100 candidates, nuanced enterprise search, agent tool-result filtering

Why it stands out. +7.94% vs Cohere Rerank v3.5 across 93 datasets and +12.70% on MAIR; supplying instructions adds up to +11.48% on real-world datasets. In Voyage's published evaluation, it improved quality on top of every first-stage method tested.

Considerations. Adds a serving hop (~$0.0025 per 100-document request) and cross-encoder latency — rerank only your top-k candidates.

rerank-2.5-lite
Active Reranker

Overview. The latency-optimized sibling, with the same 32K context and instruction following at $0.02/M tokens.

What's different
Outperforms the best open-source reranker (Qwen3-Reranker-8B) while being more than 10x smaller
Best fit
Latency-sensitive production search, high-QPS consumer applications, cost-optimized pipelines
Example workloads
Real-time search ranking, chatbot retrieval refinement at scale

Why it stands out. +7.16% vs Cohere Rerank v3.5 at roughly $0.001 per typical request — most of the flagship's lift at lite-tier latency and price.

Considerations. Averages about 0.7–0.8 points behind rerank-2.5; choose 2.5 when accuracy matters more than latency.

05 · Rerankers

Rerankers: the simplest accuracy upgrade in RAG

A reranker is a cross-encoder that scores query–document pairs jointly, refining the top results from any first-stage search (vector or BM25). It's the fastest way to lift an existing pipeline — no re-indexing, no migration, one additional API call.

When a reranker makes sense

Precision@k is your metric

Your LLM only sees the top 3–10 chunks. A reranker reorders the top ~100 candidates so the right ones make the cut — directly reducing hallucination.

You can't touch the index

Reranking layers on top of any existing stack — BM25, OpenAI, Cohere, or Voyage embeddings — with one API call. No re-embedding, no migration.

Relevance is nuanced

Instruction following (2.5 series) lets you encode business rules in plain language: prioritize titles, prefer regulatory documents, disambiguate "Jaguar" as the car brand.

A detail worth knowing: in Voyage's rerank-2.5 evaluation, the Voyage rerankers improved quality on top of all four first-stage methods tested — BM25, OpenAI v3-large, voyage-3-large, and voyage-3.5 — whereas not all rerankers on the market preserve quality when the first stage is already strong.
Versus prompting an LLM to rerank: Voyage's research position ("The Case Against LLMs as Rerankers," Oct 2025) is that purpose-built cross-encoders beat prompted LLMs on quality, latency, and cost — rerank-2.5-lite outperforms Qwen3-Reranker-8B while being over 10x smaller.

Latency / cost tradeoffs & recommended pairings

How to think about the tradeoff

  • Cross-encoders cost more compute per pair than vector lookup → rerank only your top 50–100 candidates.
  • rerank-2.5: choose when accuracy is paramount (legal, medical, finance, agent reliability).
  • rerank-2.5-lite: choose for user-facing latency budgets and high QPS — about 60% lower token price, with most of the lift.
  • The 32K context means long documents rerank without truncation.

Pairings that work well

  • Maximum accuracy: voyage-4-large documents + rerank-2.5. Voyage embeddings + Voyage rerankers is the highest-accuracy combination in Voyage's published evaluations.
  • Cost-optimized: voyage-4-lite queries (asymmetric over a 4-large index) + rerank-2.5-lite.
  • Incremental adoption: keep your current embeddings and add rerank-2.5 first — it's the lowest-effort, immediately measurable upgrade.
  • Hybrid search: BM25 + vector union → rerank-2.5 as the unifying scorer.

07 · Upgrade Guide

On an older model? Here's the upgrade path

Voyage's stated policy: each new generation is strictly better — quality, context length, latency, and throughput — and same-tier upgrades keep the same price. One note: switching embedding models normally requires re-embedding your corpus, except within the Voyage 4 shared embedding space, where models are interchangeable.

voyage-3-large→voyage-4-largeBetter accuracy at $0.12 vs $0.18; MoE serving; tops RTEB
voyage-3.5 · voyage-3→voyage-4Same $0.06 tier, near-3-large quality, joins the shared embedding space
voyage-3.5-lite · voyage-3-lite→voyage-4-liteSame $0.02 tier, higher accuracy, asymmetric-retrieval eligible
voyage-multimodal-3→voyage-multimodal-3.5Adds video, +3–5% quality, Matryoshka dimensions — same pricing
voyage-02 · voyage-01 · voyage-lite-01 · voyage-lite-01-instruct · voyage-lite-02-instruct→voyage-4 seriesDeprecated v1/v2 pilots — please migrate
Future-proofing: the Voyage 4 shared embedding space ends the "model upgrade = re-embed everything" cycle. If you index on voyage-4-large today, you can swap query models across the family — including the free open-weight nano — with zero migration. Reranker upgrades never require re-indexing at all.

Practical migration tips

  • Re-embedding a large corpus? Use the Batch API for a 33% discount with a 12-hour completion window.
  • Validate before switching: embed a representative sample with the new model and compare retrieval quality on your own queries — the 200M-token free tier typically covers this.
  • Embeddings from different models (or generations) are not compatible — queries and documents must come from the same model, except within the Voyage 4 family.

  • Try it free: every current model includes 200M free tokens — enough to embed a substantial corpus and run a real evaluation. Quickstart: docs.voyageai.com/docs/quickstart-tutorial
